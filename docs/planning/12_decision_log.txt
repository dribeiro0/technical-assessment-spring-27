ENROUTE - PRODUCT DECISIONS
Planning baseline 0.1 - November 24, 2026

DEC-001  Explain MBTA service conditions in terms of disruptions, vehicle locations and the effects on a rider's trip.
DEC-002  Serve both everyday commuters and transit enthusiasts.
DEC-003  Open the assessment dashboard on Live Map on desktop and phone. In the longer-term product, offer both network exploration and personal commute views on either device, with the network overview emphasized on desktop and saved departures on mobile.
DEC-005  Model the MBTA network across modes. Deliver Red, Orange, Blue and Green subway coverage first.
DEC-006  Base the experimental Chaos Score on passenger impact. Version the method and calibrate its weights against observations before fixing them.
DEC-007  Evaluate service conditions at network, route and commute levels.
DEC-008  Use official MBTA predictions first and clearly label scheduled fallback times.
DEC-009  Distinguish official alerts from irregularities detected in observed service. Track recovery and count correlated evidence once.
DEC-010  Design My Commute around familiar journeys, including saved departures, relevant alerts and monitoring while waiting and riding.
DEC-011  Provide rider-facing analytics and separate internal research analysis. Publish historical trends only where observations support them.
DEC-012  Collect historical observations on a schedule independent of site visits.
DEC-013  Centralize ingestion so features share the same underlying transit observations.
DEC-014  Track subsystem health separately, retain usable data during failures, recover automatically and record gaps.
DEC-015  Allow use without an account and make location access optional.
DEC-019  Preserve the map's line filter when switching views. Use feed evidence for stop labels, distinguish empty results from failed refreshes and retain the last successful data and timestamp on failure. Compare all in-scope subway lines in Analytics and combine the Green branches into one bar.

ENGINEERING DECISIONS - November 24, 2026
DEC-020  Build the web app with Vite, React and TypeScript in a web/ folder. Use React Router for views, React Leaflet with Esri's light gray basemap for the map (DEC-039), TanStack Query for data, Recharts for charts, Tailwind for styling and Vitest for tests.
DEC-021  Run Sprints 1-3 without a backend. The browser calls the MBTA V3 API directly (CORS allows any origin) without an API key. Add a Vercel serverless proxy that holds a key only if rate limits are hit in practice or streaming is built.
DEC-022  Deploy to Vercel with a custom domain registered at GoDaddy. Keep the vercel.app URL as a fallback link. Live at https://enrouteboston.vercel.app; the custom domain comes later.
DEC-023  Poll vehicles every 15 s, pause while the tab is hidden, allow at most one manual refresh per 5 s and wait until the rate-limit reset after an HTTP 429. Fetch routes once per visit and cache shapes on the device for 24 h.
DEC-024  Show a vehicle normally when its last update is up to 2 min old, faded with its age from 2 to 10 min, and drop it after 10 min. Count shown vehicles in the chart. Mark the feed stale 45 s after the last successful fetch. Show an error panel with Retry when the first load fails.
DEC-025  Keep the line filter in the URL (?line=) so it survives view switches and reloads.
DEC-026  Exclude Mattapan from the assessment release. Add it as its own line in Sprint 9.
DEC-027  Add route lines from /shapes, an auto-refresh pause toggle and a vehicle list view in Sprint 3. Updated November 25: the list lives in the sidebar instead of its own view (DEC-038).
DEC-028  Deliver each sprint as a branch and pull request into the fork's main. Submit one final pull request to the assessment repository.

DESIGN AND AUDIT DECISIONS - November 25, 2026
DEC-029  Name the app EnRoute. The planned domain enroute.boston is not bought yet; the site runs at https://enrouteboston.vercel.app.
DEC-030  Visual identity "Paper Panels": a navy frame (ribbon, tab bar, status bar), paper-white panels, a cream map and a burnt-orange brand colour. Use the official MBTA line colours, with Orange deepened to #BF6900 so markers reach 3:1 contrast on the map. Fonts: Mona Sans for text and Monaspace Neon for times and IDs, GitHub's open-source typefaces (changed from IBM Plex Sans and JetBrains Mono on November 25).
DEC-031  Layout: the navy icon ribbon, tab bar and status bar frame a sidebar and the map. The sidebar leads with four line-status badges, which also filter the map, followed by the train list. Train details float over the map as a card (a bottom sheet on phones), replacing the fixed properties column.
DEC-032  The selected-train card shows a countdown to the next stop and a strip of the stops ahead, with the line filled up to the train. Times come from MBTA predictions for that trip: one request when a train is selected, then every 15 s while it stays selected. Without predictions the card falls back to the stop text.
DEC-033  Name trains by destination ("Alewife", "C · Cleveland Circle") in map tags, the list and cards. Car and vehicle numbers appear only as details.
DEC-034  Markers glide to each new position over one polling interval so trains keep moving between updates. Jumps over 2.5 km snap instead, and gliding is off under reduced motion.
DEC-035  Line status comes from official alerts. Suspensions and shuttles count as a major disruption, any other alert of severity 3 or more as minor issues, and lower-severity notices are ignored. This is not the Chaos Score.
DEC-036  Every MBTA request shares one rate-limit wait. After an HTTP 429, all requests pause for 60 s, because the MBTA does not expose its reset header to browsers.
DEC-037  Judge train ages against the last successful fetch, not the clock, so a paused or failing feed keeps its last trains instead of emptying the map.
DEC-038  Cut the breadcrumb bar and search from the release. The sidebar list replaces the separate vehicle-list view of DEC-027.
DEC-039  Use Esri's light gray basemap, which needs no API key. CARTO, the first choice, began requiring one.
DEC-040  Tapping a station on the map, or a stop on a train strip, opens a station card with the next departures from that station, grouped by line and destination. The data is one predictions request when the station is opened, then every 15 s. A station and a train are never selected at the same time. Each car of a Green Line train is reported as its own trip, so departures from the same platform at the same moment count as one train.
DEC-041  My Commute (Sprint 4) keeps saved commutes in this browser only (localStorage key enroute.commutes.v1): no account, nothing sent anywhere. Every stored field is checked on read and a malformed entry is dropped. At most 10 commutes.
DEC-042  Commute departures use live predictions whenever any exist. The timetable (/schedules) is requested only when there is no live train, and the two lists are never mixed, because live subway trips often carry different IDs from timetable trips and mixing would show the same train twice. Each time is labelled LIVE or SCHEDULED.
DEC-043  "Leave in N min" is departure time minus walk minus buffer, rounded down so the rider is never late. When live times are stale (45 s, DEC-024), the countdown becomes clock times and no leave-time advice is given.
DEC-044  Timetable requests follow the MBTA service day: before 3 a.m. Boston time they ask for the previous day with the hour plus 24, so trains running after midnight are not reported as missing.
DEC-045  Direction names ("toward Alewife") are stored in domain/lines.ts from /routes direction_destinations instead of fetched, to save requests. They need updating only if a terminus changes.
DEC-046  On a phone, a rider with a saved commute opens on My Commute, once per visit and only from the plain landing page. Desktop and shared links such as ?line=Red still open on the map. The commute form asks /routes?filter[stop]= once per chosen station, so only the branches that serve it are offered.
DEC-047  My Commute is a card on the map, not a tab (replaces the tab in DEC-046). A separate page felt unnatural next to the rest of the app, where everything happens on the map. A station card offers "Save as my commute" and opens the form in place; saved commutes are listed in the sidebar; opening one (?commute=) shows its card where the train and station cards appear and rings its station. A train, a station and a commute are never selected together. On a phone, a rider with a saved commute opens with its card as the bottom sheet.
DEC-048  The walk to the station comes from the rider's location when they turn it on, and from their typed usual walk otherwise. The estimate is straight-line distance x 1.3 / 80 m per minute, rounded up, with no estimate beyond 3 km. Location is asked for only when the rider presses "Use my location", is never stored or sent, and only the choice to use it is remembered. A routed walking time was rejected because it would send the rider's location to a mapping service.
DEC-049  Opening a commute focuses the map on the stretch it rides, as the line filter does for a whole line: the map zooms to that stretch (leaving room for the card), draws it bold, fades every other line and station, and shows only trains on its route, direction and branch. The stretch is cut out of the full line shapes between the two stations (or to the end of the line without a destination). Choosing a line filter closes the commute.
DEC-050  Where the Red Line splits, branch membership comes from a short table of branch-only stations (domain/lines.ts), not from geometry: Braintree trains run beside Savin Hill's platform without stopping, so distance alone puts it on both branches, and the API has no per-branch stop list. The same table makes a commute's departures, advice and map trains count only trains that reach its destination (no Ashmont trains for Quincy Center). Before this, the destination was display-only and the advice could point at a train that never reaches it.
DEC-051  A commute counts a train only if it reaches the destination: besides the branch rule (DEC-050), a train whose headsign names a station before the destination (a short trip such as "JFK/UMass" for North Quincy) is left out. When the headsign is not a known station, the train counts, because hiding a real train is worse than showing a doubtful one.
DEC-052  On wide screens the map's loading and out-of-date notices sit top-left: the train, station and commute cards float top-right and covered them.
DEC-053  Sprint 5 alerts live on the map, not on a separate Alerts or Overview page (after the My Commute tab felt unnatural, DEC-047). The sidebar lists MBTA alerts under line status: in effect now, then upcoming. Choosing one opens an alert card (?alert=) and the map lights what it affects: closed stations get a warning ring, suspensions light the track between their first and last affected station on each branch.
DEC-054  Alerts are requested active and upcoming in the same single request a minute. Each is classified by its active periods: upcoming alerts are listed with their dates but do not change today's line status. Order is by passenger impact: in effect now, then major, then severity, then start. Dates read like the MBTA's own: an end before 4 a.m. counts as the night before.
DEC-055  An alert affects a commute when it suspends service on the commute's stretch in its direction, closes the boarding or destination station, or covers the whole route. A station closed mid-trip does not, because the train runs through it. When the stretch is not known yet, the card says it cannot tell; when alerts fail to load, it says so. Neither is shown as "no impact", and the status bar shows "-" rather than 0.
DEC-056  Alerts leave the sidebar, which had become crowded (replaces the sidebar list in DEC-053). A warning button with a count of alerts in effect now sits with refresh and pause (left column on desktop, top bar on phones) and opens the list as a card over the map (?alert=all); each alert card links back to it. Stations with an alert in effect now always carry a small dashed warning ring on the map, and a station's card lists the alerts that name it.
