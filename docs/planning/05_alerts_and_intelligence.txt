1. TWO KINDS OF DISRUPTION INFORMATION                     [ACCEPTED]
======================================================================

  OFFICIAL NOTICES (from MBTA /alerts)
    suspensions, station closures, shuttles, detours, planned work,
    elevator/escalator outages, delay notices

  DETECTED IRREGULARITIES (our own analysis)
    unusually long gaps, bunching, large schedule deviation, unstable
    predictions, less service than scheduled (when verifiable), abnormal
    waits

  Rule: a detected irregularity is never presented as an MBTA
  announcement. Labels, wording, and placement keep the two apart.

  Planning called this the hybrid Alert Engine: unlike predictions,
  alerts combine official and independent evidence, keeping origins
  visible.

3. PASSENGER-IMPACT PRIORITY                               [ACCEPTED]
======================================================================

Order by impact, not publication time. Suspensions and closures first,
minor delays and informational notices last.

Impact factors [PROPOSED; weights UNVALIDATED]:
  disruption type; share of route affected; duration so far;
  replacement service available; passenger exposure; accessibility
  impact (an elevator outage can block a journey entirely for some
  riders); time of day.

Relevance levels for a saved journey:
  DIRECT     the journey's segment, stop, or direction is affected
  INDIRECT   same route, outside the journey's segment, may cause delay
  NONE       unrelated route with no connecting effect
  UNKNOWN    the data needed to judge relevance is missing; never shown
             as NONE

4. LIFECYCLE
======================================================================

  UPCOMING --start--> ACTIVE --change--> UPDATED --+
                         |                         |
                         +<------------------------+
                         |
                     notice ends
                         v
                     RESOLVED (notice closed)
                         |
                 observed service back to expected?
                    no -> RECOVERY (still degraded per our metrics)
                    yes ->
                         v
                     ARCHIVED (kept for history)

  - UPCOMING notices are shown but do not count toward current chaos.
  - A notice expiring does not prove headways recovered. RECOVERY lasts
    until observed service is back within its normal range. Without
    enough observation to judge, show "recovery not confirmed" rather
    than "normal".
  - Every transition is stored with its time (07) so recovery durations
    can be studied later.

5. EXPECTED VS OBSERVED (TRANSIT INTELLIGENCE)            [ACCEPTED]
======================================================================

  Source anchor (David): "Pairing it with the brainstorm about us
  creating our own predictions, because the robot's given to us and
  what we find changes, right?" - then "I agree." to the resulting
  plan.

  EXPECTED                         OBSERVED
  schedules                        vehicle positions / stop events
  official predictions             observed arrivals and gaps
  official alerts                  travel times between stops
          \                         /
           v                       v
        COMPARISON: discrepancies, gaps, bunching, prediction drift
                       |
          +------------+-------------+
          v            v             v
     detected     prediction     Chaos Engine
     irregularity reliability    inputs
          |
          v
     history -> better thresholds, confidence, future research

  Detect only where evidence supports it. Each detection records the
  evidence used (e.g. "observed gap 14 min vs scheduled 6 min at Park
  Street northbound, 3 consecutive vehicles") and a confidence level.

  Staged ambition [PROPOSED]:
    1 show official info  2 compare with observations  3 detect
    unreported irregularities  4 learn prediction error  5 statistical
    confidence  6 experiment with independent predictions  7 forecast
    disruptions. Stages 6-7 only if earlier stages show real benefit.

7. DEDUPLICATION AND DOUBLE COUNTING                       [ACCEPTED]
======================================================================

  One incident may produce a suspension notice, a shuttle notice, an
  elevator notice, long gaps, and missing departures. These are linked
  into one incident where evidence supports it (shared entities,
  overlapping times, explicit references). The Chaos Engine counts the
  incident's passenger impact once (03 section 5). Replaying the same
  feed must not create duplicate history entries or repeat in-app
  alerts (08).

======================================================================
9. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
======================================================================

  - An active suspension is listed above an active minor delay.
  - An upcoming notice is shown as upcoming and does not change the
    current score.
  - A detected irregularity is labelled as Chaos Meter's own detection,
    with its evidence, and never with MBTA wording or branding.
  - A saved commute shows a notice only if relevance is DIRECT or
    INDIRECT, and shows which.
  - After a notice closes, the route shows "recovery not confirmed"
    until observed headways return to normal or data is insufficient.
  - If /alerts is unavailable, the UI says alerts are unavailable; it
    never says "no disruptions".
